01 · Motivation

Propagation cannot tell when it is wrong

SAM-style trackers predict each frame from the image and a memory of their own past predictions. The recursion is what makes them strong, and what makes them fragile: a single wrong mask, written into memory during an occlusion, a scene cut or a pass by a look-alike, is read back by every later frame. Better memory design postpones this. Nothing in the loop detects it or undoes it.

Our premise is that robust tracking needs a second capability next to propagation: knowing when propagation should no longer be trusted, and recovering the target when it should not.

Rows: ground truth, SAM 3, Dual-Agent. SAM 3 loses the violinist and never recovers; Dual-Agent detects the loss, relocalizes her and tracking continues.

02 · Method

Two agents on top of a frozen tracker

Dual-Agent separates two questions that propagation conflates: is the track still correct? and where is the target? A verification agent answers the first; a correction agent, invoked only on a detected failure, answers the second. A committed correction re-prompts SAM 3, so propagation continues from a clean state rather than a corrupted one.

Trigger sparsely

The agents run only when SAM 3's own signals suggest trouble: during occlusions, shortly after a reappearance, and when the tracker's confidence becomes unstable over a short window, an early sign of identity drift. The verifier is reached on 4.8% of frames.

Verify against the tracker's own history

A zero-shot VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking) sees two trusted exemplars of the target, four context frames carrying the tracker's own, possibly wrong, boxes, and the query frame. It is asked to locate the target first and only then judge the tracker's box, which makes the decision about identity rather than box quality.

Correct only when needed

A lighter VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) fine-tuned on failures mined from SAM 3's own runs, augmented with identity-switch and distractor perturbations, relocalizes the target or declares it absent. On held-out queries it gains +0.59 IoU where SAM 3 has lost the target and slightly hurts where SAM 3 is already right, which is why the verifier gates it.

Commit conservatively

A correction overwrites SAM 3's memory only if it differs substantially from the current hypothesis. One wrong commit corrupts many future frames; one right reset recovers a long segment. On SecVOS, 40 of the 52 checkable commits improve on SAM 3.

04 · Quantitative results

Consistent gains, and they transfer

With SAM 3 frozen, Dual-Agent improves it on both VOS benchmarks, and most where identity must be re-established: the SecVOS gain grows from +1.0 on single-shot videos to +4.1 on videos with multiple scene changes. Removing the verifier returns the system to the SAM 3 baseline (74.1 vs 74.2), so the gain comes from detecting failures, not from conservative acceptance.

Limitations

  • Dual-Agent is only as reliable as its verifier: 9 of 52 checkable commits on SecVOS overrode a correct track.
  • It is not real-time: about 72 s per SecVOS video on one H100 against 12 s for SAM 3; FP8 verification and sparser polling bring this to 48 s at 76.4 J&F.
  • LaSOT-ext uses a size-selected composite; on VOT-LT recall rises while precision drops.
