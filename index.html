The problem

Propagation feeds its own errors back into memory

Recent trackers improve propagation with better memory, motion cues or distractor-aware memory, yet all still trust their last prediction. One wrong mask in memory, and every later frame inherits it. Nothing in the loop notices, so nothing undoes it.

The idea

Robust tracking needs more than better propagation: it needs failure detection and recovery

Dual-Agent adds a failure-aware layer above a frozen SAM 3. A verifier checks, from the tracker's own history, whether the track is still right; a corrector re-localizes the target when it is not. A committed correction re-prompts SAM 3 from a clean memory.

Sparse triggers call the verifier. An incorrect verdict calls the corrector. A commit gate decides whether the fix reaches SAM 3's memory.

Principle 1

Asymmetric decomposition

Whether the track is wrong and where the target is are different problems. A large model verifies; a small specialist corrects.

Principle 2

Tracker-aware verification

The verifier sees trusted exemplars beside SAM 3's own, possibly wrong, recent boxes. It judges the real trajectory, not an oracle one.

Principle 3

State-safe intervention

A wrong correction corrupts every future frame, so sparse triggers and a commit gate keep interventions rare.

Principle 4

Failure-oriented supervision

The corrector learns from SAM 3's real failures, with identity-switch and distractor perturbations that mimic the ambiguities that break propagation.

How it works

One correction, from trigger to memory reset

Step 1

Trigger sparsely

The verifier is called only when SAM 3's signals look wrong: during occlusion, after a reappearance, or when its confidence oscillates. A confident SAM 3 is left alone.

Step 2

Verify against the tracker's own history

Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (zero-shot) sees two exemplars, four context frames with SAM 3's boxes, and the query. It finds the target before judging the box, so the verdict is about identity. Three votes (t, t+4, t+8) decide.

Step 3

Correct only on a detected failure

A Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct corrector, fine-tuned on 79,457 SAM 3 failures, predicts whether the target is visible and where. The verifier re-checks each proposal, up to three times.

Step 4

Commit conservatively

The fix overwrites SAM 3's memory only if it differs from the current box (IoU below 0.5). SAM 3 is then re-prompted and carries on; one reset recovers a whole segment.

Results

The harder the identity problem, the bigger the gain

Gains are largest where propagation is weakest. On SecVOS, built around scene cuts, they grow from +1.0 on single-shot videos to +4.1 on videos with multiple scene changes. On LVOS-v2, Dual-Agent improves an already strong SAM 3 and posts the best unseen-object scores.

Where the SecVOS gain comes from

Dual-Agent rarely intervenes, and lands where propagation failed: it touches 4.8% of frames, with 88 commits across 160 videos (67 better than SAM 3, 19 worse, 2 unchanged).

Transfer

Dual-Agent transfers: other trackers, other tasks

Verification and recovery are not tied to SAM 3. Swap the propagator, retrain nothing: every tracker gains, the weaker ones most, even SeC, which already reasons semantically.

Limits

When Dual-Agent makes it worse

19 of the 88 commits on SecVOS lowered the score. Three of them:

Hallucinated recovery during an absence

J&F 82.9 → 46.7

During the interior shots, SAM 3 correctly outputs nothing. At frame 57 the verifier votes INCORRECT and the corrector boxes a target that is not there; Dual-Agent paints the dashboard for 80 frames.

Overriding a correct track with the wrong object

J&F 85.0 → 65.8

SAM 3 is back on the returning red car. At frame 204 the verifier rejects this correct box; the corrector picks the black car next to it, propagated for 36 frames.

Wrong absence at a reappearance

J&F 95.8 → 87.8

Both track the singer. At frame 168 the verifier flags the track and the corrector declares her absent; Dual-Agent outputs nothing for 13 frames.
